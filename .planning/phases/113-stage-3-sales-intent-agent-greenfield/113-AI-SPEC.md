## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Extraction — ranked intent classification (closed-list enum + open-set snake_case proposal), single Orq call with strict json_schema. Not generative, not conversational, not agentic-with-tools: one email in, one ranked verdict out.

**Description:**
A sales-specific Stage 3 agent reads every sales email that survives the Stage 1 noise filter at `verkoop@smeba.nl` (Moyne Roberts / Smeba — a Dutch industrial fire-safety equipment supplier, ~33 mails/day) and classifies it into a ranked list of business intents (e.g. quote request, purchase order, order-status question, complaint), each with a confidence and short reasoning, plus a swarm-agnostic `entity_relationship` axis (resolved customer vs. likely prospect vs. unknown third party) fed by Stage 2 NXT resolution. The audience is inside-sales staff who today triage this mailbox by hand. Verdicts surface in a Bulk Review console and — after an operator green-light — route to a Kanban human-lane; there are NO auto-actions yet (Stage 4 reply handlers arrive in V11.0). "Good" = the verdict an experienced inside-sales colleague would assign at a glance, with the genuinely-ambiguous and the high-consequence (complaints, urgent orders) reliably surfaced rather than buried, and no email ever silently disappearing.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
<!-- The 3-5 behaviors that absolutely cannot go wrong in this system -->
1. **Silent dead-letter.** An email leaves Stage 1 but never reaches a visible Stage 3 verdict / human-lane row (Zod-gate throw swallowed, handler-less intent dropped instead of routed-with-reason). A lost purchase order or complaint is direct lost revenue and a damaged customer relationship. STAGE3-03: zero silent dead-letters is the hard contract.
2. **Confident misclassification of a revenue/relationship-critical intent.** A purchase order read as a generic question, or a complaint read as a routine order-status query, with high confidence and no hedge. A fluent-but-wrong top-1 looks healthy to monitoring and gets actioned downstream — the most dangerous production failure mode for intent classifiers.
3. **Vocabulary drift / out-of-enum output.** The agent returns an intent string not in the codegen'd `swarm_intents` enum, or a noise key that belongs to Stage 1 (`agent_intent` rows) — breaking the 3-way enum invariant (TS union ⇄ registry ⇄ Orq json_schema) and the hard noise-vs-intent separation. Forces a non-routable verdict.
4. **`other`/catch-all over-collapse.** Real, separable intents dumped into the residual `other` bucket because the prompt or vocabulary under-discriminates — hiding demand signal the operator needs and starving the V11.0 handler-prioritisation signal.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** B2B sales-inbox triage for an industrial distributor — Moyne Roberts / Smeba, a Dutch supplier of fire-safety equipment. Adjacent practitioner field: customer-service / inside-sales email triage. Primarily Dutch-language mail, with some English (and occasional German/French given the cross-border Smeba/Berki/Sicli footprint).

**User Population:** Inside-sales staff at Smeba (a handful of non-technical operators, per the 5–15 employee user model). They currently read and sort `verkoop@smeba.nl` by hand (~33 mails/day, ~827 over 30d). One operator reviews verdicts in the Bulk Review console during the dry-run observation window; after green-light, the wider inside-sales team works the Kanban human-lane. Salespeople otherwise keep living in Outlook (the 112.5 model) — the AI is a triage assistant, not a replacement workflow.

**Stakes Level:** High. No money moves and nothing is auto-sent in this phase (verdicts are advisory, human-in-the-loop). But the mailbox carries live purchase orders, quote requests with deal value, and customer complaints — a mis-triaged or dropped PO/complaint is direct lost revenue or a damaged customer relationship. The ceiling is High rather than Critical only because a human still sees every routed row before any customer-facing action.

**Output Consequence:** The ranked verdict + `entity_relationship` axis lands on a Bulk Review row and (post-green-light) a Kanban human-lane card with a reason. An inside-sales colleague picks it up and replies/acts in Outlook. Top-1 intent is the headline that decides which lane/priority the email lands in; the ranked tail and confidence tell the operator where to look first. Handler-less intents must route to the human-lane *with a clear reason* — never disappear. There is no automated downstream side-effect in V9.0 (Stage 4 handlers = V11.0); the consequence is entirely "did the right human see the right email in the right lane, fast enough."

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Top-1 intent matches the inside-sales read**
Good: The headline intent is the one an experienced inside-sales colleague would assign at a glance — a PO email tagged as order placement, a price/availability ask tagged as quote request.
Bad: A purchase order classified as a generic question, or two genuinely different intents (quote vs. order) swapped — the kind of error that sends the email to the wrong lane.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Inside-sales practitioner judgment; intent-detection accuracy on real (not curated) tickets is the headline triage metric.

**Dimension: Complaint / dissatisfaction is never buried**
Good: An unhappy-customer email (defective extinguisher, late delivery, billing dispute) surfaces as a complaint/escalation intent or at minimum ranks high enough that the operator sees it; tone/frustration is not lost under a routine label.
Bad: A complaint flattened into "order_status" or "other" and dropped to the bottom of the queue.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Triage best practice — complaints, disputes, safety, and escalation signals should route to a human owner before any routine handling; fire-safety product complaints carry safety overtones.

**Dimension: Urgency separated from intent**
Good: Urgency is emitted as its own axis (low/normal/high) so an urgent in-stock order and a routine catalogue question with the same intent get different priority.
Bad: Urgency collapsed into the intent label, so a "need it on site tomorrow" order looks identical to a "just checking" one.
Stakes: High
Source: Triage practitioners keep Intent, Urgency, and Risk as three separate signals — conflating them mis-prioritises the queue.

**Dimension: Customer-vs-prospect axis is right (entity_relationship)**
Good: A known NXT customer is marked resolved_entity; an unrecognised sender that looks like a new buyer is flagged as a likely prospect/unknown third party — matching what Stage 2 resolution found, honestly reflecting "unresolved" when Stage 2 is dry-run.
Bad: A first-time prospect mislabeled as an existing customer (or vice-versa), mis-routing follow-up and skewing the prospect pipeline.
Stakes: High
Source: Inside-sales practice — handling differs sharply between existing-account orders and net-new prospect enquiries.

**Dimension: Calibrated confidence and honest `other`**
Good: Genuinely ambiguous mail gets medium/low confidence (so the operator knows to look) and unclassifiable mail honestly returns `other` with reasoning; clear mail gets high confidence.
Bad: Uniformly high confidence including on ambiguous mail (false certainty), OR real separable intents over-collapsed into the `other` catch-all to look "decided."
Stakes: High
Source: Production-classifier practice — confidently-wrong verdicts look healthy to monitoring yet mislead the human; an over-stuffed catch-all hides demand signal needed for V11.0 handler prioritisation.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- **Silent dead-letter / lost order.** An email leaves Stage 1 but no visible verdict or human-lane row results (validation throw swallowed, handler-less intent dropped). In a sales inbox this is a lost PO or an unanswered complaint — the single worst outcome (STAGE3-03 exists to forbid it).
- **Confidently-wrong top-1 on a revenue/relationship-critical email.** A fluent high-confidence misclassification of a PO or complaint passes monitoring and gets actioned in the wrong lane. Worse than an honest "I'm not sure," because nothing flags it for review.
- **Catch-all over-collapse.** Separable, recurring intents land in `other` because the vocabulary or prompt under-discriminates — the same failure that the v8.1 retro found is upstream Stage-1 noise, but at Stage 3 it manifests as a bloated residual bucket hiding real demand.
- **Language / quoted-thread confusion.** Dutch mail with an English quoted thread (or an internal forward carrying the real customer message in the quote) leads to mis-read intent or wrong language tag. Internal forwards from own-tenant domains hold the real sender context in the quoted body — must be read, not filtered (MEMORY: internal forwards are context).
- **Noise-vs-intent leakage.** A Stage-1 noise concept (auto-reply, OOO, the 8 `agent_intent` AP-noise keys) reappearing as a Stage 3 intent, violating the hard one-registry-only separation and producing a non-routable verdict.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No sector-specific regulatory regime governs intent *classification* of inbound sales mail (no HIPAA/financial-advice/legal-advice analog applies — nothing is auto-replied or auto-actioned in this phase). The relevant constraints are organisational/data-handling, already enforced by the platform rather than this agent:
- **GDPR / personal data.** Sales emails contain personal and customer data; all storage stays in Supabase under RLS (service-role writes, no anon), LLM calls route only through the Orq.ai Router (never a direct provider key), and the model is Anthropic via **Bedrock EU** for data-residency. No new PII surface is created by this phase.
- **No external data egress beyond the sanctioned Orq.ai Router path.** Per CLAUDE.md hard rules — no direct OpenAI/Anthropic keys, knowledge stays in Supabase.
- **Fire-safety product domain:** complaints may carry safety implications; this is a *triage-priority* consideration (route complaints to a human promptly), not a formal compliance obligation on the classifier itself.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Orq.ai (agents + LLM Router) — accessed via the `/v2/agents/{key}/responses` REST endpoint on the hot path and `@orq-ai/node` for agent CRUD; orchestrated by Inngest.

**Version:** `@orq-ai/node` 4.7.7 · `inngest` 3.54 · `zod` (mandatory output validator). Orq API surface: `/v2` (Agents + Deployments). Primary model `list_models`-validated at planning (Anthropic via Bedrock EU — Haiku 4.5 default, Sonnet 4.5 escalation).

**Rationale:**
- **Locked, non-negotiable stack.** CLAUDE.md mandates Orq.ai as the sole LLM access path — no direct OpenAI/Anthropic keys, knowledge stays in Supabase, all model calls route through the Orq Router. There is no decision to make at the framework layer; the only honest engineering work is *how* to use Orq well, captured in Sections 3–4.
- **Proven in-tree precedent.** The debtor Stage 3 `debtor-intent-agent` runs this exact shape today: a single blocking `/responses` call, strict `json_schema` structured output, ranked-intent-v3 payload, Zod-gated on the client. This phase reuses the **generalized shared coordinator** (Phase 110 D-07) unchanged (STAGE3-01) — the only net-new Orq surface is one `orq_agents` row (`sales-intent-agent`) + its Studio json_schema. Adopting any other framework would mean re-implementing the shared classifier/dispatcher the requirement explicitly forbids touching.
- **Requirement names it.** STAGE3-01 specifies "a new Orq `sales-intent-agent`, registered in `orq_agents`" — the framework is a requirement constraint, not a design choice.
- **System-type fit.** This is single-call Extraction (ranked closed-list enum + one open-set proposal). Orq's strict `json_schema` response_format + LLM Router fallback chain is the production-reliable way to get valid enum-constrained JSON (prompt-only ≈ 15–20% failure); Inngest supplies the durable, replay-safe orchestration and the zero-dead-letter guarantee around the call.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| **LangGraph (TS, model-agnostic, Postgres checkpointing)** | The only technically viable alternative if the Orq stack lock were lifted — documented for honesty, not recommended. Would require direct provider keys (CLAUDE.md hard violation), a parallel orchestration runtime alongside Inngest, and a from-scratch re-build of the shared coordinator/dispatcher (STAGE3-01 forbids edits there). No upside for a single-call extraction task already solved by the debtor path. |
| **Direct Anthropic/OpenAI SDK** | Forbidden by CLAUDE.md (no direct provider keys); loses the Router fallback chain, EU data-residency routing, and Orq experiments/datasets/tracing this eval strategy depends on. |
| **Bare prompt-only JSON (no `response_format`)** | ~15–20% structured-output failure rate observed; incompatible with the 3-way enum invariant (TS union ⇄ `swarm_intents` ⇄ json_schema). Rejected at the model-config layer, not the framework layer. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Yes — consciously, per the CLAUDE.md non-negotiable stack. Orq.ai is the single sanctioned LLM gateway for the whole platform; this phase deepens an already-accepted, organization-wide lock-in rather than introducing a new one. The mitigation that keeps it tolerable: the *output contract* (the Zod `salesIntentOutputSchema` + the `swarm_intents` registry + codegen enum) is owned in-tree and provider-agnostic, so the model behind the Router can change (Haiku → Sonnet → a different vendor in the Orq catalog) without touching downstream code.

### Entry Point Pattern
```typescript
// Sales Stage 3 = the SAME shared coordinator (debtor-email-coordinator.ts, generalized in
// Phase 110 D-07). This phase writes NO new classifier function (STAGE3-01: zero edits to
// shared classifier + dispatcher). The only new code is the sales invoke-intent + sales agent
// row in orq_agents + swarm_intents seed. The per-email Orq call shape is the proven one:

const AGENT_KEY = "sales-intent-agent";                 // registered in orq_agents
const ORQ_ENDPOINT = "https://api.orq.ai/v2/agents";
const TIMEOUT_MS = 45_000;                              // > Orq's 31s internal retry window

async function invokeSalesIntentAgent(input: InvokeInput) {
  const apiKey = process.env.ORQ_API_KEY;
  if (!apiKey) throw new Error("ORQ_API_KEY not set");

  const body = {
    message: {
      role: "user",
      parts: [{ kind: "text", text: buildUserMessage(input) }], // XML-tagged context + assembled body
    },
    // `variables` carry email_id for cross-system trace-join (Orq ↔ Inngest ↔ agent_runs).
    configuration: { blocking: true, variables: { email_id: input.email_id, /* … */ } },
  };

  const res = await fetch(`${ORQ_ENDPOINT}/${AGENT_KEY}/responses`, {
    method: "POST",
    headers: { Authorization: `Bearer ${apiKey}`, "Content-Type": "application/json" },
    body: JSON.stringify(body),
    signal: AbortSignal.timeout(TIMEOUT_MS),
  });
  if (!res.ok) throw new Error(`Orq sales-intent-agent failed: ${res.status} ${await res.text()}`);

  const json = await res.json();
  const raw = json.output?.[0]?.parts?.[0]?.text ?? "";
  // Strip ```json fences, JSON.parse, THEN Zod-gate (never trust server-side schema alone).
  const parsed = JSON.parse(raw.trim().replace(/^```(?:json)?\s*/i, "").replace(/\s*```$/i, ""));
  return salesIntentOutputSchema.parse(parsed);  // throws → Inngest surfaces it → Bulk Review retry
}
```

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Orq Agent (`orq_agents` row + Studio config)** | A named, versioned LLM config: primary model + fallback chain + `model.parameters.response_format` (strict json_schema). Invoked by `key`. | One row per swarm classifier (`sales-intent-agent`). Set `swarms.stage3_coordinator_agent_key` to it. |

| **LLM Router + fallback chain** | Orq routes to primary; on failure retries the fallback list (≈31s window). | Set primary (Anthropic via Bedrock EU) + fallbacks. Validate every ID with `list_models` before create/PATCH. |
| **Ranked-intent V3 output shape** | `{ ranked: [...], language, urgency, intent_version, intent_proposal, proposal_reason }`. | **Codegen intent enum (3-way agreement)** | `swarm_intents` rows ⇄ generated TS literal union ⇄ Orq json_schema enum must agree. | After any vocabulary change: ONE migration + `npm run codegen` + json_schema update, atomic commit (D-04). |

### Common Pitfalls
1. **`create_agent` silently drops `model.parameters.response_format`.** Create with a bare model id, then immediately PATCH the full `model.parameters` (incl. strict json_schema), then `get_agent` to verify persistence. (CLAUDE.md learning `cba7352b`.)
2. **Unknown model IDs are accepted without error and break Studio.** PATCH echoes a bad ID; `get_agent` confirms it; Studio then renders an empty model dropdown and blocks "Add tool". ALWAYS `list_models` and validate primary + every fallback before create/PATCH. The catalog has NO `anthropic/claude-sonnet-4-6` — what the project calls "4.6" is `aws/eu.anthropic.claude-opus-4-6-v1`. (Learning `f980a2a1`.)
3. **`anyOf` for nullable, not the array shorthand.** `{"type":["string","null"]}` is valid JSON Schema 2020-12 but Orq's json_schema validator + OpenAI strict mode reject it ("Invalid JSON detected"). Use `{"anyOf":[{"type":"string"},{"type":"null"}]}`. Applies to the nullable `entity_relationship` axis (D-05) and `intent_proposal`/`proposal_reason`. (Learning `3970bad9`.)
4. **30s client timeout causes false failures.** Orq retries internally for ~31s; set the client `AbortSignal.timeout` to 45_000.
5. **Prompt-schema drift inflates the validation-failure rate.** Debtor saw ~12% Stage 3 failures when the deployed Studio prompt's `reasoning` cap diverged from the Zod cap. Keep the Studio prompt's stated field caps in lockstep with the Zod schema; verify after every `update_agent` with `get_agent` (persistence) AND an experiment run (behavior).
6. **json_schema *tool resources* cannot be created via MCP/SDK.** Tool CRUD is Studio-only. For the strict-output JSON Schema, follow the canonical CLAUDE.md workflow: create the JSON Schema in Studio → Tools → Add, then set Model parameters → Response Format dropdown = "JSON Schema" referencing it. (D-13.)
7. **Hard noise-vs-intent separation.** A key lives in exactly one of `swarm_noise_categories` (Stage 1) or `swarm_intents` (Stage 3). The 8 sales `agent_intent` noise keys (migration `20260520_phase84`) must NEVER appear in the sales `swarm_intents` seed.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
- **Primary:** Anthropic via Bedrock EU — anchor on the live `debtor-intent-agent` config. The exact ID is `list_models`-validated at planning; per CLAUDE.md the candidates are `aws/eu.anthropic.claude-haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0` (Haiku — high-volume classifier default; debtor Stage 3 runs Haiku) or `aws/eu.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929` (Sonnet — escalation/quality). Start on Haiku; escalate to Sonnet only if the eval pass-rate demands it.
- **Fallback chain (validate each via `list_models` first):** `openai/gpt-4o-mini`, `google-ai/gemini-2.5-flash`, `mistral/mistral-large-2411` (dated pin; NOT `mistral-large-latest`).
- **Parameters:** `temperature: 0` (classification — determinism over creativity); `max_tokens` set explicitly and bounded (the ranked array is capped at 5 entries × ~300-char reasoning ≈ a few hundred output tokens — budget ~1000 to leave headroom, never unbounded); `response_format` = strict json_schema referencing the codegen'd intent enum.

**Core Pattern:**
Single-call ranked extraction, NO tools on the agent. The agent does pure LLM reasoning; Inngest owns all orchestration and side-effects (per `docs/orqai-patterns.md` §9 — agent-as-tool is reserved for semantic tool-choice, which this is not). Flow:
1. Shared coordinator (`debtor-email-coordinator.ts`) derives `swarm_type` from the event payload, assembles the capped XML input (`<context>` metadata + `<inbound_message>`/`<quoted_thread>`), and invokes the sales agent by key.
2. Agent returns the V3-shaped ranked list + open-set `intent_proposal` (closed-list ranks + one optional snake_case proposal in the SAME call — D-11) + the new nullable `entity_relationship` axis (D-05).
3. Coordinator Zod-gates the output, hoists top-1 onto `agent_runs`, persists `ranked_intents` on `coordinator_runs`, dual-writes a `pipeline_events` row, flips status `classifying → predicted`, and emits `<swarm_type>/predicted` for the dispatcher. **All of this is existing shared code — sales reuses it unchanged.**

**Tool Use:**
None on the agent. The classifier is tool-free. Downstream dispatch (Stage 3.5) reads `swarm_intents.handler_status`; handler-less intents route to the Kanban human-lane with a reason (STAGE3-03, zero silent dead-letters). Stage 4 handlers are out of scope (V11.0).

**State Management:**
- **Durable run state:** Inngest `step.run()` boundaries; every side-effect (DB writes, the Orq call) inside a step so replays are idempotent. Non-deterministic IDs (`run_id`) generated INSIDE `step.run` (CLAUDE.md / Phase 65 replay-id learning).
- **Persistence:** `coordinator_runs.ranked_intents` (full ranked list), `agent_runs` (hoisted top-1 + back-compat columns), `pipeline_events` (TELE-01 audit row). The `entity_relationship` axis persists where planning decides (`coordinator_runs` or `pipeline_events.decision_details`) — hard constraint: swarm-agnostic + nullable + no shared dispatcher edits.
- **Idempotency cache:** `findCachedOutput` keyed on `(email_id, intent_version)` — a duplicate trigger reuses the prior LLM output but still re-hoists onto the new `agent_run_id`.
- **Resolution input:** Stage 2 customer/prospect resolution is read from the live `PipelineStageContext` envelope (the Stage 2→3 contract), NOT a separate audit lookup (D-06). Dry-run Stage 2 → envelope carries "unresolved"; that is the truth.

**Context Window Strategy:**
Hard input cap (debtor uses `STAGE_3_INPUT_CAP_CHARS = 8000`) applied by `assembleInput`, which truncates the quoted thread first and keeps the inbound message intact. Truncation flag + `input_chars` surfaced into `pipeline_events.decision_details.input_size` for token-bloat monitoring. The retro corpus pass (D-01) must respect the 40k hard-cap fail-loud pattern from `select-candidates.ts`. No RAG / no retrieval — the email body + capped quoted thread is the entire context.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice. **This is a TypeScript stack — "Pydantic" maps to Zod here, the project's mandatory LLM-output validator.**

### Structured Outputs with Zod (the TS analog of Pydantic)

The output schema is a Zod object validated on the client AFTER the Orq strict json_schema enforces it server-side. Two layers of defense: server-side json_schema (catches ~98%) + client Zod gate (catches schema drift, fence wrapping, and the residual). Mirror `web/lib/automations/debtor-email/coordinator/types.ts`:

```typescript
import { z } from "zod";
import { SWARM_INTENTS_BY_SWARM } from "./intent.generated"; // codegen — source-of-truth = swarm_intents

// Closed-list enum from the registry codegen (3-way agreement: TS ⇄ swarm_intents ⇄ Orq json_schema).
const SALES_INTENT = SWARM_INTENTS_BY_SWARM["sales-email"]; // e.g. ["offerte_aanvraag","order_plaatsing",...,"other"]
const CONFIDENCE = ["low", "medium", "high"] as const;

const rankedSalesIntentEntry = z.object({
  intent: z.enum(SALES_INTENT),
  confidence: z.enum(CONFIDENCE),
  reasoning: z.string().max(300),
});

export const SALES_INTENT_VERSION = "2026-06-xx.v1" as const; // planning sets the date

export const salesIntentOutputSchema = z.object({
  ranked: z.array(rankedSalesIntentEntry).min(1).max(5),   // bound prompt blow-up
  language: z.enum(["nl", "en", "de", "fr"]),
  urgency: z.enum(["low", "normal", "high"]),
  intent_version: z.literal(SALES_INTENT_VERSION),
  // Open-set discovery rides the same call (D-11) — nullable, snake_case-constrained.
  intent_proposal: z.string().max(64).regex(/^[a-z][a-z0-9_]*$/).nullable(),
  proposal_reason: z.string().max(280).nullable(),
  // Swarm-agnostic customer-vs-prospect axis (D-05) — nullable so debtor can adopt later.
  entity_relationship: z
    .enum(["resolved_entity", "unresolved_known_pattern", "unknown_third_party"]) // exact values at planning
    .nullable(),
});

export type SalesIntentOutput = z.infer<typeof salesIntentOutputSchema>;
```

**How the framework uses it:** the Orq agent's `model.parameters.response_format.json_schema.schema` is the same shape (with `anyOf` nullables — the json_schema mirror of `.nullable()`). The codegen'd enum is injected into BOTH the Zod `z.enum(...)` and the json_schema `enum` array so they cannot drift.

**Retry logic:** the Orq Router already retries the model fallback chain internally (~31s). The classifier function runs `retries: 0` at the Inngest level — a Zod-gate failure (`.parse()` throws) surfaces immediately and the email lands in Bulk Review with the raw output attached; the operator's **retry button** is the recovery path, not an automatic loop. Log on failure: the raw response text, the Zod `error.issues`, and `intent_version`. Do NOT silently retry — a validation failure usually means prompt-schema drift (pitfall #5), which a blind retry won't fix.

### Async-First Design

- **All I/O is `async/await`** (`fetch`, Supabase, `inngest.send`). The Stage 3 call is `configuration.blocking: true` — a single awaited response, NOT streaming. **Stream is wrong here:** structured-output validation needs the complete JSON before `.parse()`; a partial stream can't be Zod-gated. Stream is for conversational UX, which this is not.
- **The one common mistake:** aliasing `inngest.send` (`const send = inngest.send`) drops the `this`-binding → `TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading '_send')`. Always call `inngest.send(...)` inline or `.bind(inngest)` (CLAUDE.md / Phase 65 `dae6276`). Mocked tests do NOT catch this — only a live smoke does.
- **Replay safety > raw async:** wrap every awaited side-effect in `step.run()`. Two awaits in one step replay together; splitting them changes replay semantics. Generate non-deterministic values (UUIDs, `Date.now()`) inside the step that consumes them.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

- **System vs. user separation:** the agent's persona, the closed-list intent vocabulary, the output contract, and the Dutch few-shot examples live in the Orq Studio agent instructions (the "system" layer, versioned with the agent). The per-email `<context>` block + assembled body is the "user" message (`buildUserMessage`). The user message carries ZERO instructions — only data — so the prompt string is deterministic per `(email_id, conversation state)` and the idempotency cache holds.
- **Few-shot:** inline in the agent instructions, sourced from real Dutch retro clusters (D-12), NOT dynamically retrieved (no RAG). Keep examples representative of the promoted vocabulary; refresh when the vocabulary changes.
- **Token budget:** `max_tokens` ALWAYS set explicitly (see §4). XML-tagged sections (`<role>`, `<task>`, `<constraints>`, `<output_format>`, `<examples>`) per `docs/orqai-patterns.md` §4. Positive framing ("classify into ONE of the listed intents") over negative. Field caps in the prompt MUST match the Zod caps (pitfall #5).

### Context Window Management

Extraction, single-pass — no conversation history accumulation, no agent compaction. The bounded inputs are: (1) inbound message (kept intact), (2) quoted thread (truncated first under the 8000-char cap), (3) Stage 2 resolution status (a short envelope field). For the retro corpus pass: batch per the 40k hard-cap fail-loud selector; if a single email exceeds the cap, truncate-and-flag rather than silently dropping. No reranking needed — there is no retrieved set to rerank.

### Cost and Latency Budget

- **Per-call estimate (Haiku 4.5 via Bedrock EU):** input ~2-4k tokens (capped body + instructions), output ~300-600 tokens. At Bedrock Haiku pricing this is well under ~$0.01/email; the 827-email retro corpus is a one-time pass of a few dollars. Sonnet (escalation) is ~5-8× that — reserve it for cases Haiku's eval pass-rate can't clear.
- **Caching:** exact-match idempotency cache via `findCachedOutput` keyed on `(email_id, intent_version)` — duplicate triggers (manual replay, upstream redelivery) skip the LLM call entirely. Bumping `intent_version` auto-invalidates the cache (a vocabulary change must re-classify). No semantic cache needed — every email is distinct content; exact-match on email_id is the right grain.
- **Cheaper-model routing:** the whole classifier is already the "cheap routing model" tier (Haiku). Stage 1 noise filtering (regex + a small LLM 2nd-pass) runs BEFORE Stage 3, so Stage 3 only ever sees post-noise inbound mail — the corpus is pre-narrowed, keeping volume (and cost) down. Reserve Sonnet strictly for measured quality gaps, not as a default.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

> Question answered: *"How do we know the sales-intent-agent is working — that the right human sees the right email in the right lane, fast enough, with nothing silently lost?"* The dimensions below are the Section-1b domain rubric ingredients turned into measured criteria. Measurement leans **Orq-native** (experiments + datasets + tracing over the registered agent) backed by **Supabase telemetry** (`agent_runs`, `coordinator_runs`, `pipeline_events`) and the **Bulk Review** operator surface — no new platform account where Orq already covers it. Promptfoo is the only added tool: a CLI prompt-regression gate for the enum/schema contract that needs no account and runs in CI.

### Dimensions

| # | Dimension (domain ingredient) | Rubric — PASS / FAIL | Measurement Approach | Priority |
|---|------------------------------|----------------------|----------------------|----------|
| D1 | **Zero silent dead-letter** (STAGE3-03, failure mode #1) | **PASS:** every post-Stage-1 sales email reaches a visible terminal state — a Stage 3 verdict in Bulk Review, or a handler-less intent routed to the Kanban human-lane *with a reason*. **FAIL:** an email leaves Stage 1 (`classifying`) and reaches no visible row — Zod throw swallowed, intent dropped instead of routed, status stuck `classifying` past SLA. | **Code** — reconciliation query: count post-Stage-1 emails vs. emails with a terminal `pipeline_events` row over a window; expect **0 gap**. Runs as an Inngest health check + the CI smoke. | Critical |
| D2 | **Top-1 intent matches the inside-sales read** (1b dim 1, failure mode #2) | **PASS:** headline intent is the one an experienced inside-sales colleague would assign at a glance — a PO tagged order-placement, a price/availability ask tagged quote-request. **FAIL:** PO read as generic question, or quote↔order swapped — the email lands in the wrong lane. | **Human** (operator labels gold set) → **Code** (top-1 accuracy / misroute-rate vs. labels) on the reference dataset; ongoing via **operator overrides** in Bulk Review as the live signal. Target: top-1 ≥ 0.80, **misroute-rate < 0.10** on revenue/relationship-critical intents. | Critical |
| D3 | **Complaint / dissatisfaction is never buried** (1b dim 2) | **PASS:** an unhappy-customer email (defective extinguisher, late delivery, billing dispute) surfaces as complaint/escalation in top-1 **or ranks high enough that the operator sees it**; frustrated tone not flattened under a routine label. **FAIL:** complaint collapsed into `order_status`/`other` and sunk to the bottom of the queue. | **LLM Judge** (complaint-recall judge over the verdict + email, calibrated against operator labels ≥ 0.7 corr before trusting) + **Human** review of every judge/operator disagreement. Target: **complaint top-k recall ≥ 0.95** (very low tolerance — a missed complaint is the worst miss after a dead-letter). | Critical |
| D4 | **Out-of-enum / noise-vs-intent leakage** (failure mode #3) | **PASS:** every `ranked[].intent` is a member of the codegen'd `sales-email` `swarm_intents` enum; no Stage-1 noise key (the 8 `agent_intent` AP-noise rows) ever appears. **FAIL:** any intent string outside the enum, or a noise key surfacing as an intent — a non-routable verdict that breaks the 3-way invariant. | **Code** — Zod `z.enum(...)` gate on every response (server json_schema + client double-defense) + a Promptfoo assertion over the eval set asserting `intent ∈ enum` and `intent ∉ noise_keys`. Targets: **`other`-rate ≤ ~20%** (the D-02 coverage threshold), confidence not uniformly high (entropy floor), high-confidence agreement ≥ low-confidence agreement. | High |

Target: axis-agreement ≥ 0.85 where Stage 2 resolved; honest-`null` rate ~100% where dry-run. | High |

### Reference Dataset

## 6. Guardrails

> Catastrophic-if-wrong → **online** (every request, real-time, must be fast). Quality-signal → **offline flywheel** (sampled/batch, feeds improvement). Guardrails kept minimal — each adds latency to a ~33-mails/day path where correctness, not throughput, is the constraint.

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| **Zero-dead-letter terminal-state guarantee** (D1 / STAGE3-03) | Any post-Stage-1 email that does not reach a Stage 3 verdict — Zod-gate throw, or an intent with no Stage 4 handler. | **Route, never drop.** Zod throw → Inngest surfaces it → email lands in **Bulk Review with raw output attached** + operator **retry** button (no silent auto-retry — a validation throw usually means prompt-schema drift a blind retry won't fix). Handler-less intent → **route to Kanban human-lane with the reason**. The hard contract: every path ends in a visible row. |
| **Strict enum / schema gate** (D4 + D5) | Response fails `salesIntentOutputSchema.parse()` — out-of-enum intent, noise-key leakage, drifted field cap, malformed nullable. | **Block the bad verdict** from reaching a terminal "predicted" state; surface raw output to Bulk Review for operator/retry. Server-side `json_schema` (≈98%) + client Zod (residual + fence/drift) — two layers. |
| **Complaint floor** (D3) | LLM-judge (or a lightweight rule on complaint-signal terms) flags a likely complaint that the verdict ranked low / buried under a routine label. | **Flag + force-surface** in Bulk Review (and, post-flip, route to the human-lane regardless of top-1) so a frustrated/safety-tinged email is never sunk. Tuned for recall over precision. |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Top-1 / misroute-rate vs. operator overrides** (D2) | Weekly batch over all operator overrides in Bulk Review during the dry-run window, then a smart sample post-flip (weight to overridden + low-confidence rows). | Misroute-rate > 0.10 on critical intents → inspect the misrouted cluster, refine prompt few-shots (Dutch retro examples) or split an over-broad intent; re-run the Orq experiment before re-PATCH. |
| **`other`-rate & coverage drift** (D6 / D8) | Batch over each `intent_version` window. | `other`-rate climbing above ~20% or a recurring separable cluster inside `other` → feed `intent-proposals-refresh`; operator promotes a new intent (data-driven), then the atomic migration + `npm run codegen` + json_schema enum update (D-04). |
| **Confidence calibration** (D6) | Batch correlate emitted confidence vs. operator-agreement per window. | Uniformly-high confidence (false certainty) or high-confidence ≈ low-confidence agreement → prompt-calibration pass; consider Sonnet escalation only if Haiku can't clear it. |
| **Entity-axis agreement** (D7) | Batch vs. the Stage 2 envelope resolution status on resolved-customer mail. | Agreement < 0.85 → inspect prompt-context wiring of the resolution envelope; honest-`null` must hold while Stage 2 is dry-run. |
| **Open-set proposal yield** (STAGE3-02) | Continuous via `intent_proposals_v1` / `intent_proposal_clusters` after go-live (D-11). | Healthy proposal stream feeds the vocabulary; a stall or a flood signals prompt or threshold tuning. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** **Orq.ai tracing** (every `/responses` call, joined to Inngest runs + `agent_runs` via the `email_id` carried in `configuration.variables`) as the model-layer trace, durably mirrored to **Supabase** (`coordinator_runs.ranked_intents`, `agent_runs`, `pipeline_events` — TELE-01 audit row incl. `decision_details.input_size`/truncation flag). The **pipeline-health monitor** (VERIFY-01) carries the sales ingest/throughput signal. No external observability platform — the durable eval truth is the Supabase trio queried by SQL.

**Key Metrics to Track:**
1. **Dead-letter gap (D1) = 0** — post-Stage-1 emails minus emails with a terminal `pipeline_events` row, per window. The single most important number; any non-zero is a STAGE3-03 breach.
2. **Misroute-rate on critical intents (D2)** — from operator overrides; headline triage-quality metric (target < 0.10).
3. **Complaint top-k recall (D3)** — fraction of true complaints surfaced high enough to be seen (target ≥ 0.95).
4. **Validation-failure rate (D4/D5)** — Zod-gate throws / total calls (target < 2%; watch for the prompt-schema-drift spike pattern that hit debtor at ~12%).
5. **`other`-rate & coverage (D6/D8)** — residual-bucket share per `intent_version` (target ≤ ~20%).
6. **Cost / latency / token drift** — per-call tokens (capped body via the 8k input cap), Haiku-tier cost, p95 latency under the 45s timeout. Cheap-routing tier; alert on drift, not on level.

**Alert Thresholds (page / operator-notify):**
- **Page:** dead-letter gap **≥ 1** in any window (catastrophic, STAGE3-03). Validation-failure rate **> 5%** sustained (prompt-schema drift in production). Any out-of-enum or noise-key leakage reaching a terminal state (**> 0** — should be impossible past the gate).
- **Operator-notify (non-paging):** misroute-rate > 0.10 on critical intents; complaint recall < 0.95; `other`-rate > 20%; entity-axis agreement < 0.85; p95 latency approaching the 45s ceiling or token-per-call drift > 1.5× baseline.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
Weight human review toward the interactions most likely to be wrong (course guidance — don't sample uniformly):
- **All operator-overridden rows** — the strongest live ground-truth signal (and the D-10 axis-3 correction source).
- **Low / medium confidence verdicts** — where the model itself signals doubt (D6).
- **Anything the complaint-judge flagged** but the verdict ranked low (D3) — the catastrophic-miss early-warning.
- **`other` verdicts** — to catch under-discrimination feeding the vocabulary flywheel (D8).
- **Validation-failure / retry rows** — the raw-output-attached Bulk Review rows (D5) — drift detector.
- **Signal-metric divergence:** when operator-override volume rises while top-1-accuracy metrics still look healthy, investigate manually — the early warning that the eval set has gone stale against evolving mail.
